# Evaluation hardening: what the numbers really say

Scripts: `scripts/make_dataset.py` (leak filtering, validation split), `scripts/leakage_check.py`, `scripts/evaluate.py`.
All numbers are for the scam classifier at threshold 0.5 unless stated. Intervals are bootstrap 95% CIs (1,000 resamples).
Full figures are in `reports/eval.json`, which is regenerated by the pipeline.

## 0. Update: current numbers (17 Oct, after the harder synthetic data, the real-world set and real legit training ads)

Sections 1–5 below were written before the synthetic data was made harder (hard negatives and positives, shared sign-offs, every third phrase held out), before `data/handwritten.csv` existed, and before weakly labelled real legit ads were added to training. They are kept as the record of what was found and fixed. These are the current figures, from `reports/eval.json` and `scripts/leakage_check.py` on `main`.

| | Section 1 (before) | Now |
|---|---|---|
| Overall F1 / ROC-AUC | 0.833 / 0.970 | **0.809 [0.786, 0.833] / 0.940 [0.929, 0.950]** |
| EMSCAD F1 / recall | 0.729 / 0.619 | 0.736 / 0.628 |

**Leakage now.** No near-duplicates of train remain in test, shifted or the real-world set (0 rows above cosine 0.9). 73% of synthetic test lines still also occur in train, and **99% of synthetic validation lines** do, so validation stays optimistic: val AUC 0.998 against test 0.942.

**Shortcuts now (synthetic train, legit / scam share).** Fictional agency names (50% / 46%) and licence numbers (24% / 15%) appear in both classes; no licence number is single-class any more. The only single-class token left is **Island Star Recruiters** (scam only, 95 rows), which is deliberate: it is the suspended demo agency. Rupee amounts stay more common in scams (46% vs 22%). That is a real signal, because scams ask for fees.

**Masking ablation.** Masking agency names, licence numbers, phones and amounts changes 57% of test rows. F1 moves from 0.809 to 0.790 and AUC from 0.940 to 0.925, so the model barely leans on those tokens.

**Cross-domain (F1 / AUC).**

The two domains still don't transfer. Synthetic scores measure our generator; the real-world set is the only Sri Lankan reality check.

## 1. What was inflated

| | Before | After (honest split) |
|---|---|---|
| Test rows | 1,941 | 1,698 |
| Overall F1 | 0.828 | 0.833 [0.811, 0.854] |
| Overall ROC-AUC | 0.975 | 0.970 [0.963, 0.977] |
| **EMSCAD F1** | 0.812 | **0.729 [0.653, 0.796]** |
| **EMSCAD recall** | 0.738 | **0.619 [0.529, 0.708]** |
| Synthetic F1 | 0.832 | 0.852 [0.827, 0.872] |
| Synthetic recall | 0.993 | 0.993 [0.985, 1.000] |
| **Precision if 5% of offers are scams** | not reported | **0.26 [0.23, 0.29]** |
| **Precision if 1% of offers are scams** | not reported | **0.06 [0.05, 0.07]** |

The overall F1 barely moves, but that hides two changes in opposite directions. The test set lost a quarter of its EMSCAD rows, and those rows were the easy ones.

- **Near-duplicate leakage (EMSCAD).** 244 of the ~940 EMSCAD test ads (26%) were near-copies of training ads (char n-gram cosine > 0.9; the same company re-posting the same text). Removing them drops EMSCAD F1 from 0.81 to 0.73 and recall from 0.74 to 0.62. On real ads that are new to the model, it catches about 6 in 10 scams.
- **Unrealistic base rate.** The test set is 33% scam. Real traffic is more likely to be 1–10% scam. At 0.5 the model flags 14% of legitimate offers (FPR). If 5% of offers were scams, only about 1 in 4 alerts would be a real scam, with 134 false alarms per 1,000 offers. If 1% were scams, about 1 in 16 alerts would be real.
- **Sri Lankan legitimate offers are over-flagged.** On synthetic rows the false-alarm rate is 27% (EMSCAD: 1.5%). Precision there would be 0.03–0.04 at a 1% base rate.

## 2. Leakage and shortcut findings (`scripts/leakage_check.py`)

- **Near-duplicates:** 244 test rows and 95 candidate validation rows were near-copies of train, all EMSCAD. They are now dropped from test or moved back into train. Synthetic rows had **0** near-duplicates and only 4 shared a whole-text skeleton. Because the synthetic CSVs carry no template id, the split groups rows by a masked text skeleton.
- **Phrase reuse is the real synthetic leak.** The generator recombines phrases. 65% of synthetic test lines (and 99% of synthetic val lines) also appear in train. Only 33% of synthetic test rows have half or more of their lines new. On those rows confidence falls (0.84 vs 0.91), F1 is 0.83 and AUC 0.98. The model still separates the classes there, but every test offer was still produced by the same generator.
- **Label shortcuts in the synthetic train data:**
  - *Licence numbers appear only in legit ads* (22% of synthetic legit, 0% of scams). Every licence number is single-class. A scammer can quote a fake licence number to look legitimate.
  - *Island Star Recruiters* appears only in scams (29 rows). Every other agency appears in both classes.
  - Mobile placeholder `07X XXX XXXX`: legit 46%, scam 54%. Amounts in `Rs.`: legit 29%, scam 56%. `example.lk` e-mail: legit 20%, scam 8%. Agency name present: legit 40%, scam 19%.
- **Masking ablation.** Agency names, licence numbers, phone placeholders and amounts were masked in 56% of test rows. Test F1 does not drop: 0.833 unmasked, 0.837 masked. Synthetic AUC falls from 0.985 to 0.969. Retraining and testing on masked text gives F1 0.828. So these tokens are a weak crutch, not the main thing the model relies on. The shortcut risk is in phrasing (see above), not in the placeholders.

## 3. Cross-domain ablation: the domains do not transfer

| Train on | Test EMSCAD (F1 / AUC) | Test synthetic (F1 / AUC) |
|---|---|---|
| EMSCAD only | 0.79 / 0.97 | **0.57 / 0.71** |
| Synthetic only | **0.30 / 0.61** | 0.86 / 0.99 |
| Both (shipped model) | 0.73 / 0.97 | 0.85 / 0.99 |

What the model learns from US job ads barely carries over to Sri Lankan offers, and the reverse is close to chance. The 0.99 synthetic AUC therefore tells us the model has learned *our generator*. It says nothing about real Sri Lankan scams. A real hand-written test set is the only way to measure that. `evaluate.py` scores `data/handwritten.csv` as TEST-ONLY once it exists.

## 4. Thresholds (chosen on VAL, checked on TEST; `config.py` unchanged)

| Operating point | Threshold | Test precision | Test recall | Test FPR | Precision at 5% scams |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Current MEDIUM_RISK | 0.40 | 0.73 | 0.94 | 0.17 | 0.22 |
| VAL best F1 | **0.58** | 0.79 | 0.91 | 0.12 | 0.29 |
| Current HIGH_RISK | 0.70 | 0.84 | 0.88 | 0.08 | 0.36 |
| VAL recall ≥ 0.9 | **0.80** | 0.91 | 0.84 | 0.04 | 0.51 |

The validation set is optimistic (val AUC 0.994 vs test 0.970) because its synthetic rows share 99% of their phrases with train. As a result, the "recall ≥ 0.9" threshold picked on val (0.80) gives only 0.84 recall on test. Suggestion for the team: keep about **0.4 as the human-review threshold** (test recall 0.94). Consider **raising HIGH_RISK towards 0.8** if fewer high-risk false alarms matter more than recall, and do not trust either threshold until it has been checked on the real hand-written set.

## 5. Honest headline

> On held-out data with near-duplicates removed, the model scores F1 0.83 [0.81, 0.85] and ROC-AUC 0.97 [0.96, 0.98]. On real (EMSCAD) ads it catches 62% [53%, 71%] of scams. At a realistic 5% scam rate, about 1 in 4 alerts at threshold 0.5 is a real scam (1 in 2 at 0.8). Sri Lankan performance is measured only on synthetic offers from the same generator, so it is an upper bound.

## Unresolved

- No real Sri Lankan test data yet (`data/handwritten.csv` was not present when this was written).
- The validation split cannot be made phrase-novel without changing `generate_synthetic.py` (for example, a third reserved phrase pool for validation).
- Confidence intervals cover sampling noise only, not the gap between synthetic and real offers.
